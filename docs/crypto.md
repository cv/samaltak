# Cryptography

`System-Crypto` (layer 0) is cryptography in Smalltalk, for the TLS 1.3 client where the VM has no OpenSSL (UEFI, docs/uefi.md): hashes, MACs, key derivation, authenticated encryption; the public-key half (X25519, RSA and ECDSA signatures) is its own part of the same package. Tests: `Tests-Crypto`, against the standards' own vectors.

## API

- **Hashes** (`CryptoHash`): `SHA256`, `SHA384`, `SHA512`. `SHA256 hash: bytes` → a ByteArray; incremental `SHA256 new update: bytes; update: more; digest`; `digest` finishes a copy, so the hash goes on (a TLS transcript's running hash), and `copy` forks it. Class side `digestSize` and `blockSize` (bytes). A String is hashed as its UTF-8.
- **HMAC** (RFC 2104): `HMAC hash: SHA256 key: k message: m`, or incremental `(HMAC hash: SHA256 key: k) update: m; digest`.
- **HKDF** (RFC 5869): `HKDF hash: SHA256 extract: salt ikm: ikm` → the PRK, `HKDF hash: SHA256 expand: prk info: info length: n` (n at most 255 blocks).
- **AEAD**: `(ChaCha20Poly1305 key: k) encrypt: plaintext nonce: n aad: a` → the ciphertext with the 16-byte tag appended; `decrypt:nonce:aad:` → the plaintext, or `CryptoError` if the tag does not verify (checked in constant time before anything is decrypted). Class side `keySize`, `nonceSize`, `tagSize`.
- **AEAD with AES**: `(AesGcm key: k) encrypt:nonce:aad:` and `decrypt:nonce:aad:` the same way (NIST SP 800-38D), keys of 16, 24 or 32 bytes; a 12-byte nonce as TLS uses (others are hashed into the first counter block).
- **Primitives under the AEADs**: `ChaCha20 key: k nonce: n counter: c` (`xor:`, `xor:from:to:into:at:`, `nextBlock`), `Poly1305 key: k message: m` (incremental as HMAC), `Aes key: k` (`encryptBlock:`, `decryptBlock:`).
- **Randomness**: `SecureRandom default nextBytes: n`; `SecureRandom platformEntropy: n` for the raw source.
- **Errors**: `CryptoError` (an Error): a tag that does not verify, keys and nonces of the wrong size, a ChaCha20 counter that wraps, HKDF beyond its limit, no source of entropy or a stuck one.
- **Tests**: FIPS 180-4, RFC 4231, RFC 5869, RFC 8439 (every vector of 2.x and A.1–A.5), FIPS 197, McGrew and Viega's 18 GCM test cases, and Project Wycheproof's AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305 vectors with short messages (`WycheproofAeadTest`: 244 and 173, a third of them invalid: modified tags, wrapped counters, nonces of other sizes); tampering with any part signals CryptoError.
- **Bytes**: `ByteArray>>constantTimeEquals:` compares MACs and tags in time that depends on their sizes only; `ByteArray fromHex:` reads test vectors.
- **Primitives in the VM** (`NativeCrypto`, the VM's `CryptoPrimitives`): SHA-256 and SHA-512 compression of whole blocks, AES encryption of blocks and in counter mode (GCM's 32-bit counter), GHASH; `SHA256`, `SHA384`, `SHA512`, `Aes` and `AesGcm` (so HMAC, HKDF and TLS) call them first, and their Smalltalk, the fallback and the reference, runs where a primitive fails (a VM without them, arguments they refuse) or after `NativeCrypto enabled: false`. `NativeCrypto features` → the bits of `featureNames` (`#aes #ghash #sha256 #sha512 #crc32 #crc32c`) whose work the CPU's instructions do, the rest being portable C; `NativeCrypto useFeatures: mask` limits them (0: the portable C alone) and answers the previous mask. `Crc32` and `Crc32c` (System-Storage; PNG's chunks, ext4's checksums) use the CRC primitives the same way (`tableUpdate:bytes:from:to:` is the Smalltalk). `NativeCryptoTest` runs the vectors' test classes again each way (Smalltalk, portable C, instructions) and compares the ways on random inputs.

## How

Everything is SmallInteger arithmetic, no LargeInteger in any loop: SmallIntegers have 63 bits (signed, docs/implementation.md), and the JIT's optimizing tier (docs/jit.md) compiles `bitAnd: bitOr: bitXor: bitShift: + * at: at:put:` on them inline, with the working words in temps.

- SHA-256 and ChaCha20 work on 32-bit words. A 32-bit word shifted left by up to 30 bits still is a SmallInteger, so a rotation is a shift right ored (or xored) with a shift left masked to 32 bits; SHA-256 xors the three left shifts of Σ0 and Σ1 first and masks once, and masks sums of up to five words once. ChaCha20's twenty rounds are unrolled over sixteen temps; its key stream is xored a 32-bit word at a time (`uint32At:`, little-endian as ChaCha20).
- SHA-512's 64-bit words do not fit: each is two 32-bit halves, a rotation by n < 32 takes each half's top bits from the other half (by n ≥ 32 they swap first), and a sum carries the low halves' bits above 32 into the high one.
- Poly1305's accumulator and r are five 26-bit limbs (poly1305-donna's 32-bit layout): a product of limbs is below 2^56 and a sum of five below 2^60. The final reduction modulo 2^130 - 5 picks h or h - p by masks, not by a branch.
- AES works on little-endian column words, as `uint32At:` reads them and as GCM's counter mode xors them: each round is sixteen lookups in four tables of 256 words (SubBytes, ShiftRows and MixColumns of a column at once), built from the S-box, itself computed from GF(2^8)'s inverses, when the class initializes; decryption is the equivalent inverse cipher. GHASH multiplies by H with Shoup's 8-bit tables: b·H for every byte b, four big-endian words each (4 KB per key, made in about 12 µs), and what a shift by a byte reduces back in at the top, so a block is sixteen lookups, shifts and xors.
- `SecureRandom` is a ChaCha20 DRBG with fast key erasure: each request is ChaCha20's key stream under the current key, whose first 32 bytes become the next key. Where its entropy comes from, and how it is checked: Randomness, below.
- Nothing here is hardened against timing beyond the tag comparisons and Poly1305's last step: the JIT may branch where the source does not, and table-driven AES leaks through the cache, as table AES does everywhere.
- The primitives' kernels are C in `CryptoPrimitives class>>cPreamble`: portable (SHA-256 and SHA-512 as FIPS 180-4 writes them, AES with one table of SubBytes and MixColumns, GHASH by Karatsuba with BearSSL's constant-time 64-bit carry-less multiplication, CRCs by slicing-by-8), and with the CPU's instructions where it has them: on arm64 the Armv8 cryptographic extensions (AESE/AESMC eight blocks at a time in CTR, PMULL, SHA256H/H2/SU0/SU1, SHA512H/H2/SU0/SU1, CRC32X and CRC32CX), on x86-64 AES-NI, PCLMULQDQ, SHA-NI and SSE4.2's CRC32 (CRC-32C only). Each such kernel enables its instructions for itself alone (a target attribute: the VM's default target, and UEFI's, has none) and runs once the CPU says it has them: the auxiliary vector on Linux, `ID_AA64ISAR0_EL1` on UEFI, CPUID on x86-64; every Apple arm64 CPU has them. GHASH works on each byte's bits reversed, so that a block is a 128-bit little-endian polynomial, reduced modulo x^128 + x^7 + x^2 + x + 1 by two multiplications by 16r87. With the instructions AES and GHASH no longer leak through the cache.

## Randomness

`SecureRandom` makes every TLS key share, nonce and client random, the session token and the display password, so it must neither be weak nor repeat.

- **The generator.** A ChaCha20 DRBG with fast key erasure (each request answers ChaCha20's key stream under the current key, zero nonce, after its first 32 bytes, which become the next key: a key never serves twice and the state does not reveal what was answered). Its first key is SHA-256 of two 32-byte blocks of the platform's entropy; after each MB answered, SHA-256 of the key and a fresh block. Requests are serialized by a Semaphore, so Processes share `SecureRandom default`. It runs in Smalltalk on the boot CPU alone (UEFI's application processors run only the VM's C: raster bands, the JIT's back end), so there is no state shared with them. The interpreter and both JIT tiers answer the same bytes for a fixed source (checked by SHA-256 of 200 requests under `--jit none`, `baseline` and `opt`).
- **Where the entropy comes from.** On an operating system `/dev/urandom` (FileStream). On UEFI the firmware's `EFI_RNG_PROTOCOL`, GetRNG with its default algorithm; QEMU's firmware (AAVMF on arm64, OVMF on x86-64) offers it only over a virtio-rng device, so QEMU_UEFI adds `-device virtio-rng-pci`, whose bytes are the host's `/dev/urandom`: both offer one algorithm, `EFI_RNG_ALGORITHM_RAW` (e43176d7-b6e8-4827-b784-7ffdc4b68561). On real hardware `FirmwareDiagnostics` reports the protocol (`rng:` line: its algorithms, a GetRNG). Nothing weaker stands in: with no protocol, or a GetRNG that fails, `nextBytes:` signals CryptoError, and TLS with it. There is no timer or clock fallback, and the VM has no primitive for the CPU's generator (RNDR, RDRAND): the DGX Spark's cores lack FEAT_RNG (no RNDR under KVM), and QEMU's default x86-64 CPU under TCG has no RDRAND; edk2's RngDxe uses either where the CPU has it.
- **Health.** Every 32-byte block is checked as it comes from the source: one with fewer than 8 different byte values (a sound source draws one once in 10^37), or the same as the block before it, is a stuck source: CryptoError, and no output, until the source answers sound blocks again. The old key is dropped before seeding, so a failed seed never answers from it.
- **Snapshots.** A snapshot holds every generator's key; each run of the image must draw a new one, or two runs resumed from one file (or the run that wrote it and one resumed from it) would answer the same bytes. The key belongs to the image's session (`Smalltalk session`), which `processStartUpList:` renews before any class hears that the image started, so a startUp: method that makes a secret cannot get the snapshot's stream, wherever it is in the startup list. (Until this was so, SecureRandom reseeded at its own place in the list, after ControlServer's startUp:, which made the session token: every resume of a snapshot showed the same token.)
- **Checks.** `SecureRandomTest`: known answers for a fixed source, computed outside the image with an independent ChaCha20 and SHA-256 (the seed, fast key erasure, the reseed after a MB); a new session reseeds every generator, and a copy of a generator (what a snapshot keeps) does not answer its twin's bytes afterwards; a stuck or missing source is an error, also at the reseed after a MB; 80000 bits within 850 of half (6 deviations; the bound used to be 400, 2.8 deviations, which a sound generator fails once in 210 runs: that was `testBitsBalanced`'s failure on UEFI) and every byte value; 64 KB's byte histogram's chi-square below 400 (255 degrees of freedom; once in 6 * 10^7). `tests/boot/resume_rng_test.sh` (test-core) resumes a real snapshot twice with the control service: the bytes and the made token differ each time; test-uefi resumes its snapshot twice from the image store and compares SecureRandom's bytes.
- **Measured** (UEFI under QEMU, KVM on arm64 and TCG on x86-64, two boots; 256 KB of GetRNG and 1 MB of SecureRandom each): bits within 1.7 deviations of half, byte chi-square 255 to 300 (p 0.03 to 0.49), runs test p above 0.1, serial correlation below 0.004, 7.9992 bits of entropy per byte, incompressible (xz), no 16-byte block repeated within a boot or across boots. Of 1000 runs of the old 400-bit bound, 2, 6 and 4 failed (arm64, arm64, x86-64), hosted 9 of 3000: the rate of a sound generator (0.47 %).


## Performance

32 MB at once (the Smalltalk 4 MB), `--jit opt` (the default), on arm64 (an NVIDIA GB10: Armv9.2, every instruction above), hosted on Linux (gcc) and on UEFI under QEMU with KVM (clang), best of four; "Smalltalk" is `NativeCrypto enabled: false`, as before the primitives (the same within 2 %), "C" `NativeCrypto useFeatures: 0`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SHA-256 | 51 MB/s | 313 MB/s | 1.9 GB/s | 46 MB/s | 367 MB/s | 1.9 GB/s |
| SHA-512 | 41 MB/s | 470 MB/s | 1.2 GB/s | 37 MB/s | 561 MB/s | 1.1 GB/s |
| AES-128-GCM, either way | 36 MB/s (CTR and GHASH about half each) | 264 MB/s | 1.1 GB/s | 34 MB/s | 313 MB/s | 2 GB/s |
| AES-256-GCM, either way | 31 MB/s | 219 MB/s | 1.1 GB/s | 30 MB/s | 248 MB/s | 2 GB/s |
| CRC-32, CRC-32C | 307 MB/s | 2.7 GB/s | 32 GB/s | | 2.3 GB/s | 32 GB/s |
| ChaCha20-Poly1305, either way | 67 MB/s (ChaCha20 49 ms of 58, Poly1305 7) | | | 69 MB/s | | |
| SecureRandom | 77 MB/s (1 MB in 13 ms) | | | 77 MB/s | | |

Hosted AES-GCM's 32 MB answer is new memory, whose first touches cost as much as the cryptography (UEFI's heap is touched already). The kernels alone (a C harness, 4 MB): SHA-256 1.9 GB/s, SHA-512 1.2 GB/s, AES-128-CTR 3.5 GB/s, GHASH 2.5 GB/s (a block at a time; aggregating four before reducing would do more), CRC-32C 22 GB/s.

HTTPS through the Smalltalk TLS client (`TLSSocket useSmalltalkTLS: true`, 64 MB from openssl s_server -WWW on the same machine): hosted AES-128-GCM 36 → 570 MB/s, AES-256-GCM 31 → 557 MB/s, ChaCha20-Poly1305 65 MB/s either way; on UEFI (from the host, through the image's own TCP over virtio-net) AES-128-GCM 31 → 240 MB/s. A TLS record of 100 bytes takes about 7 µs with ChaCha20-Poly1305 in Smalltalk; with the primitives AES-GCM's is a few primitive calls, and a new AES-GCM key no longer builds GHASH's 4 KB table (made only if the Smalltalk GHASH runs). The client still prefers AES-128-GCM, then ChaCha20-Poly1305, then AES-256-GCM.

## Public-key cryptography

X25519 key agreement, ECDH on P-256 and P-384, and the signature verifications TLS 1.3 and X.509 need: RSA PKCS #1 v1.5 and RSA-PSS, ECDSA on P-256 and P-384. Only verification: a TLS client signs nothing. Certificates are docs/certificates.md.

- **X25519** (RFC 7748): `X25519 newPrivateKey` (32 bytes of `SecureRandom`), `X25519 publicKeyFor: priv`, `X25519 sharedSecret: priv peer: pub` (32-byte ByteArrays, little-endian as the RFC encodes them; CryptoError if the secret is all zeros, a peer key of small order, as TLS 1.3 requires); `X25519 scalar: k u: u` is the bare function.
- **RSA** (RFC 8017): `RsaPublicKey modulus: n exponent: e` (1024 to 8192 bits, else CryptoError); `verifyPkcs1: sig message: m hash: SHA256` (SHA384, SHA512; the DigestInfo is rebuilt and compared byte for byte, its NULL parameters required); `verifyPss: sig message: m hash: SHA256` (MGF1 with the same hash, the salt as long as the hash: TLS 1.3's rsa_pss_rsae_* and rsa_pss_pss_*), `verifyPss:message:hash:saltLength:` for another salt length, or any if nil (certificates give theirs in the algorithm's parameters). The `...digest:` variants take a precomputed digest. A malformed signature is false, never an error.
- **ECDSA** (FIPS 186-4, SEC 1): `EcPublicKey curve: #p256 point: bytes` (or #p384; the uncompressed point 04 || X || Y, CryptoError unless on the curve), `verify: derSignature message: m hash: SHA256` → Boolean: strict DER, r and s in [1, n-1], a longer digest truncated to the order's bits.
- **ECDH** (SEC 1, 3.3.1): `EcPrivateKey newOn: #p256` (or #p384), `publicKeyBytes` (uncompressed: the TLS key share), `sharedSecret: peerBytes` → X of the shared point (CryptoError unless the peer's point is on the curve).

**How.** Integers are the VM's LargeIntegers, whose arithmetic is primitives (32-bit limbs, schoolbook multiplication, Knuth's division), reduced modulo the prime after each product: at these sizes that beats Montgomery or special-prime reduction written in Smalltalk. X25519 is RFC 7748's Montgomery ladder, inverting by Fermat. `EcCurve` holds P-256 and P-384 (a = -3) with points in Jacobian coordinates (doubling dbl-2001-b, general addition, one inversion for the result); ECDSA computes u1·G + u2·Q with Shamir's trick. RSA verification is s^e mod n, square and multiply. **Not constant-time**: the primitives' running time and allocations depend on the values, and the ladder's swaps and the double-and-add are branches. That does not matter for verification (no secrets) and is tolerable for ephemeral key agreement against a network observer; there are no long-term private keys.

**Tests** (`Tests-Crypto`): RFC 7748's vectors (the iterated one to 1000 iterations, the Diffie-Hellman example), and subsets of Project Wycheproof (`WycheproofVectors`, generated by `tools/wycheproof-subset.py` from its JSON files: one test of each flag and comment, at most 30 a file) of x25519, ecdh_secp256r1_ecpoint, ecdsa_secp256r1_sha256, ecdsa_secp384r1_sha384 and _sha256, rsa_signature_2048_sha256, 3072_sha384 and 4096_sha512, rsa_pss_2048_sha256_mgf1_32, 4096_sha384_mgf1_48 and 2048_sha256_mgf1_0, and for the symmetric side hmac_sha256/384/512 and hkdf_sha256/384/512 (HmacHkdfWycheproofTest). The whole files pass too (4340 tests, the 'acceptable' ones left out), run once through the same calls from a script.

**Performance**, `--jit opt` hosted on arm64 (an NVIDIA GB10, Linux): X25519 0.7 ms; ECDSA verification P-256 1.2 ms, P-384 2.8 ms; RSA-2048 verification PKCS #1 v1.5 0.13 ms, PSS 0.22 ms; RSA-4096 0.5 ms.
