# Cryptography

`System-Crypto` (layer 0) is the image's cryptography: hashes, MACs, key derivation, authenticated encryption, X25519, RSA and ECDSA verification, randomness. The work is the VM's: one set of approved `vm:core` exports, `core_crypto_*` (vm/native/crypto.h), on libcrypto (the system's OpenSSL 3) hosted and on mbedTLS (vendored, vm/third_party; docs/tls.md) on UEFI; System-Crypto's classes are their facade, under the API they always had. HMAC, HKDF and SecureRandom's generator stay Smalltalk, over the exports' hashes and ChaCha20. Tests: `Tests-Crypto`, against the standards' own vectors, hosted (libcrypto) and on UEFI (mbedTLS: test-uefi runs them).

## API

- **Hashes** (`CryptoHash`): `SHA256`, `SHA384`, `SHA512`. `SHA256 hash: bytes` → a ByteArray; incremental `SHA256 new update: bytes; update: more; digest`; `digest` finishes a copy, so the hash goes on (a running transcript hash), and `copy` forks it. Class side `digestSize` and `blockSize` (bytes). A String is hashed as its UTF-8. The state of an incremental hash is the VM's, in a ByteArray of the hash object's: it cannot go on in another session of the image (a snapshot resumed: CryptoError).
- **HMAC** (RFC 2104): `HMAC hash: SHA256 key: k message: m`, or incremental `(HMAC hash: SHA256 key: k) update: m; digest`.
- **HKDF** (RFC 5869): `HKDF hash: SHA256 extract: salt ikm: ikm` → the PRK, `HKDF hash: SHA256 expand: prk info: info length: n` (n at most 255 blocks).
- **AEADs** (`CryptoAead`): `(ChaCha20Poly1305 key: k) encrypt: plaintext nonce: n aad: a` → the ciphertext with the 16-byte tag appended; `decrypt:nonce:aad:` → the plaintext, or `CryptoError` if the tag does not verify (nothing decrypted is answered). `(AesGcm key: k)` the same way (NIST SP 800-38D), keys of 16, 24 or 32 bytes, a 12-byte nonce as TLS uses (others up to 128 bytes, OpenSSL's limit and so both backends', are hashed into the first counter block; longer ones are a CryptoError). Class side `nonceSize`, `tagSize`, `maxPlaintextSize`.
- **ChaCha20** (RFC 8439, 2.4): `ChaCha20 key: k nonce: n counter: c` (`xor:`, `xor:from:to:into:at:`, `nextBlock`), each call from a block boundary: SecureRandom's generator.
- **X25519** (RFC 7748): `X25519 newPrivateKey` (32 bytes of SecureRandom), `X25519 publicKeyFor: priv`, `X25519 sharedSecret: priv peer: pub` (32-byte ByteArrays, little-endian; CryptoError if the secret is all zeros, a peer key of small order); `X25519 scalar: k u: u` is the bare function.
- **RSA** (RFC 8017): `RsaPublicKey modulus: n exponent: e` (1024 to 8192 bits, else CryptoError); `verifyPkcs1: sig message: m hash: SHA256` (SHA384, SHA512; NULL DigestInfo parameters required); `verifyPss: sig message: m hash: SHA256` (MGF1 with the same hash, the salt as long as the hash), `verifyPss:message:hash:saltLength:` for another salt length, or any if nil. The `...digest:` variants take a precomputed digest. A malformed signature is false, never an error.
- **ECDSA** (FIPS 186-4, SEC 1): `EcPublicKey curve: #p256 point: bytes` (or #p384; the uncompressed point 04 || X || Y, CryptoError unless on the curve), `verify: derSignature message: m hash: SHA256` → Boolean: strict DER, r and s in [1, n-1] (checked in Smalltalk, the same for both backends), a longer digest truncated to the order's bits.
- **Randomness**: `SecureRandom default nextBytes: n`; `SecureRandom platformEntropy: n` for the raw source.
- **Errors**: `CryptoError` (an Error): a tag that does not verify, keys and nonces of the wrong size, a ChaCha20 counter that wraps, HKDF beyond its limit, no source of entropy or a stuck one, a VM without the exports.
- **Bytes**: `ByteArray>>constantTimeEquals:` compares MACs and tags in time that depends on their sizes only; `ByteArray fromHex:` reads test vectors.
- **The backend**: `CryptoExports backend` → e.g. 'OpenSSL 3.0.13 30 Jan 2024' or 'mbedTLS 3.6.8'.

## How

- **The exports** (`FFIPrimitives-crypto`, IDs 720–730, on UEFI through `UefiFFIStubMemory-crypto`'s static wrappers; `CryptoExports` calls them): `core_crypto_digest` (one-shot), `digest_init`, `digest_update`, `digest_final` on a 256-byte state in the caller's ByteArray (OpenSSL's SHA256_CTX and SHA512_CTX, mbedTLS's contexts: plain structs, copied in and out, so a copy of the ByteArray forks the hash and `digest_final` leaves it as it was), `aead_key` and `aead` (seal and open in place, the nonce and the additional data in one slice), `x25519`, `public_key` (a DER SubjectPublicKeyInfo, which RsaPublicKey and EcPublicKey build from their numbers), `verify` (PKCS #1 v1.5, PSS with a salt length or any, ECDSA; on a digest), `chacha20` (the key stream xored in place from a block counter) and `backend`. Buffers are borrowed for the call alone.
- **Keys** (AEAD and public) are handles into the VM's table of 64 (vm/native/crypto_keys.h): when it is full the least recently used goes, and its handle (slot and generation) answers stale, so the facade makes it again; a handle from another session of the image is made again too. Nothing needs finalization.
- **Backends.** Hosted, libcrypto: the low-level SHA contexts (OpenSSL 3's `SHA256()` fetches the algorithm from a provider at every call, 3.7 times slower for 64 bytes), EVP for the AEADs (an EVP_CIPHER_CTX per key, the key schedule kept), X25519 and verification (d2i_PUBKEY once per key). On UEFI, mbedTLS 3.6 with samaltak_mbedtls_config.h: SHA-256 and SHA-512 with Armv8's instructions on arm64 (assumed, as for AES), AES-GCM with AES and PMULL (arm64) or AES-NI and PCLMULQDQ (x86-64, detected), ChaCha20-Poly1305, X25519 on its ECP module (the scalar clamped as RFC 7748 decodes it; the ladder's blinding from a hash of the inputs: no secret depends on it), pk verification.
- **CRCs** are no cryptography: `Crc32` (System-Support; PNG's chunks) keeps the VM's primitive (`CryptoPrimitives`, 646: Armv8's CRC32 instructions, else C eight bytes at a time; 647, CRC-32C for ext4, is retired); `tableUpdate:bytes:from:to:` is the Smalltalk. The SHA-2, AES and GHASH primitives (640–645) are retired, with the Smalltalk SHA-2, AES, GHASH, ChaCha20 and Poly1305 they accelerated.

## Randomness

`SecureRandom` makes every TLS key share, nonce and client random, the session token and the display password, so it must neither be weak nor repeat.

- **The generator.** A ChaCha20 DRBG with fast key erasure (each request answers ChaCha20's key stream under the current key, zero nonce, after its first 32 bytes, which become the next key: a key never serves twice and the state does not reveal what was answered). Its first key is SHA-256 of two 32-byte blocks of the platform's entropy; after each MB answered, SHA-256 of the key and a fresh block. Requests are serialized by a Semaphore, so Processes share `SecureRandom default`. It runs in Smalltalk on the boot CPU alone (UEFI's application processors run only the VM's C: raster bands, the JIT's back end), so there is no state shared with them. The interpreter and both JIT tiers answer the same bytes for a fixed source (checked by SHA-256 of 200 requests under `--jit none`, `baseline` and `opt`).
- **Where the entropy comes from.** On an operating system `/dev/urandom` (FileStream). On UEFI the firmware's `EFI_RNG_PROTOCOL`, GetRNG with its default algorithm; QEMU's firmware (AAVMF on arm64, OVMF on x86-64) offers it only over a virtio-rng device, so QEMU_UEFI adds `-device virtio-rng-pci`, whose bytes are the host's `/dev/urandom`: both offer one algorithm, `EFI_RNG_ALGORITHM_RAW` (e43176d7-b6e8-4827-b784-7ffdc4b68561). Nothing weaker stands in: with no protocol, or a GetRNG that fails, `nextBytes:` signals CryptoError, and TLS with it. There is no timer or clock fallback, and the VM has no primitive for the CPU's generator (RNDR, RDRAND): the DGX Spark's cores lack FEAT_RNG (no RNDR under KVM), and QEMU's default x86-64 CPU under TCG has no RDRAND; edk2's RngDxe uses either where the CPU has it.
- **Health.** Every 32-byte block is checked as it comes from the source: one with fewer than 8 different byte values (a sound source draws one once in 10^37), or the same as the block before it, is a stuck source: CryptoError, and no output, until the source answers sound blocks again. The old key is dropped before seeding, so a failed seed never answers from it.
- **Snapshots.** A snapshot holds every generator's key; each run of the image must draw a new one, or two runs resumed from one file (or the run that wrote it and one resumed from it) would answer the same bytes. The key belongs to the image's session (`Smalltalk session`), which `processStartUpList:` renews before any class hears that the image started, so a startUp: method that makes a secret cannot get the snapshot's stream, wherever it is in the startup list. (Until this was so, SecureRandom reseeded at its own place in the list, after ControlServer's startUp:, which made the session token: every resume of a snapshot showed the same token.)
- **Checks.** `SecureRandomTest`: known answers for a fixed source, computed outside the image with an independent ChaCha20 and SHA-256 (the seed, fast key erasure, the reseed after a MB); a new session reseeds every generator, and a copy of a generator (what a snapshot keeps) does not answer its twin's bytes afterwards; a stuck or missing source is an error, also at the reseed after a MB; 80000 bits within 850 of half (6 deviations; the bound used to be 400, 2.8 deviations, which a sound generator fails once in 210 runs: that was `testBitsBalanced`'s failure on UEFI) and every byte value; 64 KB's byte histogram's chi-square below 400 (255 degrees of freedom; once in 6 * 10^7). `tests/boot/resume_rng_test.sh` (test-core) resumes a real snapshot twice with the control service: the bytes and the made token differ each time; test-uefi resumes its snapshot twice from the image store and compares SecureRandom's bytes.
- **Measured** (UEFI under QEMU, KVM on arm64 and TCG on x86-64, two boots; 256 KB of GetRNG and 1 MB of SecureRandom each): bits within 1.7 deviations of half, byte chi-square 255 to 300 (p 0.03 to 0.49), runs test p above 0.1, serial correlation below 0.004, 7.9992 bits of entropy per byte, incompressible (xz), no 16-byte block repeated within a boot or across boots. Of 1000 runs of the old 400-bit bound, 2, 6 and 4 failed (arm64, arm64, x86-64), hosted 9 of 3000: the rate of a sound generator (0.47 %).


## Performance

Hosted, arm64 (an NVIDIA GB10, Linux, the JIT, one core), one call through System-Crypto's API: before (the image's Smalltalk with the crypto primitives and LargeIntegers) and now (the exports, libcrypto).

| | before | now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SHA-256 of 64 bytes | 0.33 µs | 0.09 µs |
| SHA-256 of 1 MB | 518 µs | 511 µs (about 2 GB/s: the CPU's instructions both) |
| HMAC-SHA256 of 48 bytes | 1.56 µs | 1.08 µs |
| AES-128-GCM seal, 16 KB | 14.9 µs | 7.9 µs |
| AES-128-GCM seal, 4 MB | 3.23 ms | 1.63 ms |
| ChaCha20-Poly1305 seal, 16 KB | 332 µs | 22.7 µs |
| X25519 shared secret | 999 µs | 97 µs |
| ECDSA P-256 verify | 1.77 ms | 89 µs |
| RSA-2048 PSS verify | 118 µs | 36 µs |
| SecureRandom, 32 bytes | 1.37 µs | 1.0 µs |

**Tests** (`Tests-Crypto`): FIPS 180-4, RFC 4231, RFC 5869, RFC 8439 (2.x and A.x), McGrew and Viega's GCM test cases, RFC 7748 (the iterated vector to 1000 iterations, the Diffie-Hellman example), Project Wycheproof's AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305 vectors with short messages (`WycheproofAeadTest`; AES-GCM nonces over 128 bytes, valid in Wycheproof, are refused), and subsets (`WycheproofVectors`, generated by tools/wycheproof-subset.py) of x25519, ecdh_secp256r1_ecpoint, ecdsa_secp256r1_sha256, ecdsa_secp384r1_sha384 and _sha256, rsa_signature_2048_sha256, 3072_sha384 and 4096_sha512, rsa_pss_2048_sha256_mgf1_32, 4096_sha384_mgf1_48 and 2048_sha256_mgf1_0, hmac_sha256/384/512 and hkdf_sha256/384/512. They pass on both backends: hosted, and on UEFI in test-uefi (arm64 and x86-64).
