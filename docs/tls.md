# TLS

HTTPS hosted is OpenSSL's, through the platform's TLS primitives (docs/platform.md); where they fail (UEFI, where the socket is the image's own TCP) it is the VM's TLS engine: mbedTLS 3.6 (vendored, vm/third_party), a TLS 1.3 and 1.2 client driven over memory buffers by `MbedTLSConnection` (System-TLS), the ciphertext moved by the image over its socket. `TLSSocket` chooses under its unchanged API, so `HTTPClient` (and server-sent events over it) works on UEFI as it does hosted. The engine's trust anchors are a `TrustStore` (docs/certificates.md); its errors `TLSError`. A TLS client in Smalltalk (TLS 1.3 and 1.2 on System-Crypto, with its own certificate validation) served UEFI before the engine and is retired.

    c := MbedTLSConnection on: (RemoteSocket connectTo: 'example.com' port: 443) host: 'example.com'.
    c handshakeUntil: Time millisecondClockValue + 10000.      "TLSError if it fails"
    c sendAll: request; receiveInto: buffer timeout: 5000; close.
    c version. c cipherSuite. c protocol.                       "'TLSv1.3' 'TLS1-3-AES-128-GCM-SHA256' 'http/1.1'"

    TLSSocket engine: #mbedtls.                                  "hosted: the engine rather than OpenSSL"
    TLSSocket trustStore: (TrustStore new add: testCA; yourself). "the engine trusts just that (nil: the default roots)"

## The engine

- **In the VM.** `vm/native/tls.c` is a client over memory buffers on mbedTLS: connections and trust stores are small integer handles (64 and 16 at a time). The VM exports it to the image as approved `vm:core` FFI exports `core_tls_*` (`FFIPrimitives-tls`, IDs 700–712: seed, set_time, store_new/add/free, new, handshake, read, write, take, close, info, free; docs/ffi.md), on UEFI through static wrappers (`UefiFFIStubMemory-tls`). Each step takes the ciphertext the image has received and room for what to send, both borrowed for the call alone: mbedTLS reads its records straight from the image's input buffer and writes them straight into its output buffer, and a read decrypts into the caller's ByteArray, so a record is copied once each way (the spike before it copied twice more). A step answers what it produced, what it consumed and flags (done, more output waiting in the engine, input wanted, failed) in one integer. The engine never calls the OS or the firmware: the image seeds its CTR_DRBG (96 bytes of `SecureRandom`, so EFI_RNG on UEFI, at the first connection of a session and again at each one; PSA's random comes from it too), tells it the time (`Time utcMicroseconds`, the firmware's clock on UEFI) before each handshake, and gives it the trust anchors (DER).
- **Configuration** (vm/native/samaltak_mbedtls_config.h, over mbedTLS's default): TLS 1.3 and 1.2, client only; suites TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256, TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384, TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256, then TLS 1.2's ECDHE-ECDSA and ECDHE-RSA with AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305 (no RSA key exchange, CBC, DHE or PSK); ALPN http/1.1 offered; the server's certificate verified for the host name (DNS and IP subjectAltNames) against the store, with mbedTLS's default profile (SHA-256 or better, RSA of 2048 bits or more, the NIST curves). AES and GHASH with the CPU's instructions (Armv8's AES and PMULL, x86-64's AES-NI and PCLMULQDQ, detected at run time on x86-64 and hosted Linux; on UEFI arm64 assumed, as SHA-256's and SHA-512's), compiled with `-march=armv8-a+crypto` on UEFI arm64.
- **MbedTLSConnection** has the protocol of a TLS connection TLSSocket uses: `handshakeUntil:` (a deadline; TLSError with mbedTLS's reason, for a certificate also why it did not verify, e.g. "The certificate validity has expired"), `sendAll:`, `receiveInto:timeout:` (0 at the end, also an end without close_notify, as TLSSocket over OpenSSL reads it), `close` (close_notify), `forget`, `isOpen`. `trustStore:` before the handshake (unless set, the roots of `TrustStoreRoots`, which the engine parses from their DER without parsing them in Smalltalk: a session's first connection costs about 0.2 s less on UEFI). The engine parses each TrustStore once a session (again if it grew). Sends are serialized; one Process receives at a time. Its handles belong to the VM that made them: in an image resumed from a snapshot a connection is closed, and the engine is seeded again.
- **TLSSocket** (`TLSSocket engine`): `#platform` (the default) uses OpenSSL's primitives where they work and the engine where they fail (the image's own TCP on UEFI, a VM without TLS primitives); `#mbedtls` the engine always. Where neither works (a VM without the engine where OpenSSL's primitives fail) connecting fails.
- **Limits** of mbedTLS 3.6 here: no KeyUpdate (a server's is an error), a Certificate message must fit its 16 KB record buffer (a 48 KB one fails the handshake), the server's chain must be in order (an issuer after its certificate), no session resumption.
- **Tests.** `TLSOpenSSLTest`: openssl s_server with a CA and certificates made per run, through the engine (each suite of both versions, HelloRetryRequest, ECDSA, a CertificateRequest, an IP address, a wrong host, an untrusted CA, expired, self-signed, incomplete, weak-key and SHA-1 certificates, long chains, 3 MB down, 2 MB up and back, 100 connections, a close from another Process, timeouts, HTTPClient and server-sent events); `TLSSocketMbedTLSTest` runs TLSSocketTest with the engine. test-uefi's HTTPS goes through it.
- **Measured** (UEFI arm64 under QEMU with KVM, the GB10): 64 MB from openssl s_server -WWW on the host at 346 MB/s (the Smalltalk client it replaced: 283 to 306 MB/s; plain TCP 496 to 688); a handshake with the host's server (P-256) 3 to 6 ms (the Smalltalk client: 47 to 64); https://inference-api.nvidia.com 23 ms after a 25 ms connect (the Smalltalk client: 79 to 82 ms). UEFI x86-64 under TCG: api.github.com through TLSSocket and HTTPClient, 200. The EFI binary grows by about 0.4 MB.

## TLSSocket

`TLSSocket connectTo:port:` connects a `RemoteSocket` and then uses OpenSSL's primitives (docs/platform.md) unless the socket is the image's own TCP (a negative handle: UEFI), `tlsStart` fails (UEFI's `UefiNoIO`), or `TLSSocket engine: #mbedtls`; then an `MbedTLSConnection` trusting `TLSSocket trustStore` (the Mozilla roots of `TrustStoreRoots` unless set). The connect timeout covers lookup, connect and handshake; a handshake failure is a `RemoteError` ("cannot connect to host:port: TLS handshake: why"); after it, a failed connection reads as ended (0) and sends answer false, as a RemoteSocket's do. Many servers close HTTP connections without close_notify: both read that as the end. Entropy on UEFI comes from the firmware's EFI_RNG_PROTOCOL (`SecureRandom`); QEMU's firmware has it with a virtio-rng device, which `make uefi-run` and the rest add.

## Tests

`Tests-TLS`, hosted: `TLSOpenSSLTest` (above, The engine), `TLSSocketMbedTLSTest` (TLSSocketTest's timeouts, cancelling, servers that are not TLS and handles left, with the engine), and the certificates' tests (docs/certificates.md). `make test-uefi` gets `https://10.0.2.2:port/` from inside QEMU, from `tools/tls-test-server.sh` (a test CA, injected into the image by the eval, and an ECDSA certificate for 10.0.2.2), through the engine.

## Real servers

`tools/tls-survey` (opt-in: it needs the internet) connects to each host of `tools/tls-survey-hosts.txt` (136: CDNs, clouds, code hosting and package registries, inference APIs, popular sites, governments and banks, CAs with RSA, ECDSA and cross-signed roots, Let's Encrypt's and badssl.com's test hosts) with `openssl s_client` (the system's roots; certificate and host verified; any version, and TLS 1.3 only) and with the image's `TLSSurvey` (the engine, Mozilla's roots: the handshake, HEAD /, read until the server closes), and prints both verdicts side by side; `-j` connections at a time, results in build/tls-survey. `TLSSurvey` also works on UEFI (`TLSSurvey new run: #('github.com') parallel: 4`), measures downloads (`download:`, the engine and OpenSSL's) and runs of connections (`repeat:host:`). Through mbedTLS: 0 mismatches of 136; both refuse expired, wrong-host, self-signed, untrusted-root and incomplete-chain servers, TLS 1.0-only ones and 1000-sans.badssl.com (OpenSSL for its chain, mbedTLS for its 16 KB record buffer); both accept revoked-isrgrootx1 (no revocation checking: OpenSSL's s_client does none either).

## Limits

Those of the engine (above): TLS 1.3 and 1.2, no KeyUpdate, Certificate messages within 16 KB, chains in order, no session resumption, PSK, 0-RTT, renegotiation, client certificates, OCSP stapling, certificate transparency or revocation (OpenSSL does not check revocation by default either), host names in ASCII.
