# TLS

A TLS 1.3 client in Smalltalk (RFC 8446), with TLS 1.2 (RFC 5246) for the servers that have nothing newer, `System-TLS`, for where the VM has no OpenSSL: UEFI, where it is the only HTTPS there is. It rests on `System-Crypto` (hashes, HMAC, HKDF, AES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, X25519, P-256, RSA and ECDSA verification, SecureRandom; docs/crypto.md) and on System-TLS's certificates (`X509Certificate`, `CertificateValidator`, `TrustStore`). `TLSSocket` uses it under its unchanged API, so `HTTPClient` (and server-sent events over it) works on UEFI as it does hosted.

    c := TLSClientConnection on: (RemoteSocket connectTo: 'example.com' port: 443) host: 'example.com'.
    c handshakeUntil: Time millisecondClockValue + 10000.      "TLSError if it fails"
    c sendAll: request; receiveInto: buffer timeout: 5000; close.

    TLSSocket useSmalltalkTLS: true.                            "hosted: the Smalltalk TLS rather than OpenSSL"
    TLSSocket trustStore: (TrustStore new add: testCA; yourself). "trust just that (nil: TrustStore default)"

## How

- **The connection.** `TLSClientConnection` runs over any connected byte socket that answers `sendAll:`, `receiveInto:timeout:` (0 at the end, nil after the timeout), `isOpen` and `close`: a `RemoteSocket`, over the OS's TCP or the image's (`NetworkStack`). It waits only on the socket, so its Process waits on Semaphores and the VM never blocks; the handshake has a deadline (a millisecondClockValue); terminating the Process that waits cancels. Before the handshake: `trustStore:` or `certificateValidator:`, `protocols:` (ALPN, `http/1.1` by default), `cipherSuites:`, `versions:` (TLS 1.3 and 1.2 by default), `keyShareGroups:`, `recordPadding:`. After it: `sendAll:` (false once the connection is gone), `receiveInto:timeout:`, `updateKeys`, `close` (close_notify), and `version`, `cipherSuite`, `keyExchangeGroup`, `protocol`, `peerCertificates`, `wasRetried`, `handshakeMilliseconds`. An error that is not a TLSError (a bug) fails the handshake as internal_error and closes the socket. One Process receives at a time; sends are serialized (a Semaphore), also with the KeyUpdates that receiving answers.
- **ClientHello** (`TLSClientHello`, which also parses one): supported_versions TLS 1.3 then 1.2, the suites TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256, TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256, TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 in that order and then TLS 1.2's (below), ec_point_formats (uncompressed), extended_master_secret and an empty renegotiation_info for TLS 1.2, SNI unless the host is an IP address, supported_groups x25519 then secp256r1 with a key share for x25519, signature_algorithms rsa_pss_rsae_sha256/384/512, ecdsa_secp256r1_sha256, ecdsa_secp384r1_sha384 and rsa_pkcs1_sha256/384/512 (in TLS 1.3 for certificates only: a CertificateVerify with them is illegal_parameter), ALPN, and a random 32-byte session id: middlebox compatibility (RFC 8446, D.4), so a change_cipher_spec goes out once before the second flight (or the second ClientHello) and the server's are skipped until its Finished.
- **The handshake** follows section 4 strictly: a HelloRetryRequest (`TLSServerHello isHelloRetryRequest`) gets a second ClientHello with a share for the group asked for and the cookie, the transcript restarting with message_hash; a second one, one for a group not offered or already shared, or one that changes nothing is fatal. ServerHello must echo the session id, choose TLS 1.3, a suite offered (the HelloRetryRequest's) and our group; then `TLSKeySchedule` (section 7.1) derives the handshake secrets from the (EC)DHE secret and the transcript hash, and records are protected. EncryptedExtensions may only answer what was offered (else unsupported_extension) and not what belongs in the hellos (illegal_parameter); ALPN must choose one of ours. A CertificateRequest is answered with an empty Certificate. Certificate (an empty request context, no entry extensions, at least one certificate) is parsed and validated for the host at the current time (`CertificateValidator`, below); CertificateVerify is checked with the leaf's key over 64 spaces, the context string and the transcript hash (RSA-PSS or ECDSA by scheme, the key's type and curve matching); Finished by HMAC, in constant time. Then the client's Finished and the application keys. Handshake messages may span records but not key changes (ServerHello and Finished end their records); messages of more than 256 KB are refused.
- **TLS 1.2**, when the ServerHello has no supported_versions and the legacy version 1.2 (`TLS12KeySchedule`, `TLS12RecordCipher`): the suites ECDHE-ECDSA and ECDHE-RSA with AES-128-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305 and AES-256-GCM (RFC 5289, RFC 7905; no RSA key exchange, CBC, RC4, 3DES, NULL or anonymous suites, so a server with only those refuses us with handshake_failure). The ServerHello must not carry TLS 1.3's downgrade sentinel (`DOWNGRD` 1 or 0 at the end of its random: someone removed TLS 1.3 from our offer) while we offer TLS 1.3, must have renegotiation_info (RFC 5746: OpenSSL 3 refuses servers without it too; handshake_failure), may only answer what was offered, must not echo our session id (a resumption of a session we never had). Then Certificate (the key type must fit the suite), ServerKeyExchange (a named curve of ours; its signature over both randoms and the parameters checked with the leaf's key: PKCS #1 v1.5, PSS or ECDSA with SHA-256/384/512, a scheme we offered, else decrypt_error), CertificateRequest perhaps (an empty Certificate answers it), ServerHelloDone; our ClientKeyExchange, the master secret by the PRF (with extended_master_secret, RFC 7627, from the session hash, as all servers seen do), change_cipher_spec and Finished; the server's change_cipher_spec, which alone turns its keys on (a Finished before it is unexpected_message), and its Finished (constant-time). Records: AES-GCM with its 8-byte explicit nonce, ChaCha20-Poly1305 with the IV XOR the sequence number, the sequence number, type, version and length the additional data. Warning alerts are ignored, a HelloRequest gets a no_renegotiation warning (no renegotiation), KeyUpdate does not exist (`updateKeys` does nothing).
- **Certificates** (`CertificateValidator`): the path from the leaf to an anchor of the TrustStore (the server's certificates in any order, cross-signed copies of anchors, at most 8 long), each certificate within its validity, the CA and key usage constraints, no unknown critical extension, the leaf for the host (subjectAltName, wildcards as browsers do). Every key must be ECDSA P-256 or P-384 or RSA of at least 2048 bits (`minimumRsaBits`, OpenSSL's default security level 2), every signature on the path one of SHA-256/384/512 (SHA-1 and MD5 are refused by name: "signed with sha1WithRSAEncryption, which is too weak"). The TLSError says which certificate failed and why, and carries the alert: certificate_expired, unknown_ca (no path to an anchor: self-signed, an untrusted root, an incomplete chain), unsupported_certificate, bad_certificate.
- **Records** (`TLSRecordCipher`, one a direction and key): at most 2^14 bytes of plaintext a record (2^14 + 256 of ciphertext received: else record_overflow), the nonce the IV XOR the 64-bit sequence number, the header the additional data, the content type after the content, zero padding stripped on receipt (optional on sending, `recordPadding:`); a record that does not decrypt is bad_record_mac. Alerts: close_notify ends the input (receiving answers 0), user_canceled is ignored, any other is fatal (`TLSError`, `isFromPeer`). Input that ends without close_notify is a TLSError (no alert), as with OpenSSL 3: between records ("without close_notify (the data may be truncated)") as well as inside one; a connection closed from another Process while one receives reads as the end (0). Many servers close HTTP connections without close_notify (15 of the 136 surveyed): `TLSSocket` reads that as the end, as before. A failure the client detects sends the alert that says why (`TLSError alert`, encrypted once there are keys) and closes the socket; a timeout or a closed connection sends none. After the handshake: NewSessionTicket is ignored (no resumption), KeyUpdate updates the server's key (and answers with ours if it asked), anything else is unexpected_message. `TLSReader` and `TLSWriter` are the presentation language (decode_error on anything short or left over).
- **TLSSocket.** `TLSSocket connectTo:port:` connects a `RemoteSocket` and then uses OpenSSL's primitives (docs/platform.md) unless the socket is the image's own TCP (a negative handle: UEFI), `tlsStart` fails (UEFI's `UefiNoIO`), or `TLSSocket useSmalltalkTLS: true`; then a `TLSClientConnection` trusting `TLSSocket trustStore` (`TrustStore default`, the Mozilla roots, unless set). The connect timeout covers lookup, connect and handshake; a handshake failure is a `RemoteError` ("cannot connect to host:port: TLS handshake: why"); after it, a failed connection reads as ended (0) and sends answer false, as a RemoteSocket's do.
- **Entropy on UEFI** comes from the firmware's EFI_RNG_PROTOCOL (`SecureRandom`); QEMU's firmware has it with a virtio-rng device, which `make uefi-run` and the rest add.

## Real servers

`tools/tls-survey` (opt-in: it needs the internet) connects to each host of `tools/tls-survey-hosts.txt` (136: CDNs, clouds, code hosting and package registries, inference APIs, popular sites, governments and banks, CAs with RSA, ECDSA and cross-signed roots, Let's Encrypt's and badssl.com's test hosts) with `openssl s_client` (the system's roots; certificate and host verified; any version, and TLS 1.3 only) and with the image's `TLSSurvey` (the Smalltalk TLS, Mozilla's roots: the handshake, HEAD /, read until the server closes), and prints both verdicts side by side; `-j` connections at a time, results in build/tls-survey. `TLSSurvey` also works on UEFI (`TLSSurvey new run: #('github.com') parallel: 4`), measures downloads (`download:`, the Smalltalk TLS and OpenSSL's) and runs of connections (`repeat:host:`).

The first survey found 12 mismatches, all ours: 11 servers without TLS 1.3 (registry.npmjs.org, www.baidu.com, www.ovhcloud.com, www.actalis.it, Let's Encrypt's valid-isrgrootx1/x2 and revoked- test hosts, badssl.com's sha256, ecc256, rsa4096 and tls-v1-2) and www.ssl.com, which closes the connection on a ClientHello without TLS 1.2 suites. With TLS 1.2: 0 mismatches. Both refuse expired, wrong-host, self-signed, untrusted-root and incomplete-chain servers and TLS 1.0-only ones; both accept revoked-isrgrootx1 (no revocation checking: OpenSSL's s_client does none either). 12 hosts negotiate TLS 1.2, 2 send a HelloRetryRequest for secp256r1 (login.microsoftonline.com, www.certum.pl). On UEFI the survey found that concurrent first lookups failed ("not on UEFI"): `NetworkStack active` now opens the interface once under a lock and the others wait for it.

## Performance

Hosted (arm64, the JIT), against openssl s_server on 127.0.0.1: a handshake with an RSA-2048 certificate 10 to 20 ms, with P-256 ECDSA 8 ms; 100 MB down (with AES-GCM on the native crypto primitives, docs/crypto.md) in 233 ms with TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 (430 MB/s), 307 ms with TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384, 197 and 161 ms with TLS 1.2's ECDHE-ECDSA AES-128-GCM and AES-256-GCM; ChaCha20-Poly1305, all Smalltalk, 1.75 s (57 MB/s; TLS 1.2: 2.05 s). From the internet (proof.ovh.net, 100 MB): 3.5 to 4.2 s with the Smalltalk TLS, 3.7 to 3.9 s with OpenSSL: the network's speed. 30 connections in a row: github.com handshakes median 83 ms, api.anthropic.com 21 ms, registry.npmjs.org (TLS 1.2) 34 ms, login.microsoftonline.com (HelloRetryRequest) 136 ms, all with the network's round trips. On UEFI under QEMU (arm64, KVM): a handshake with the host's test server 29 ms; with https://inference-api.nvidia.com (X25519, AES-128-GCM, RSA-PSS, a chain of 3) 79 ms, its connect 103 ms with the lookup, then HTTPClient's GET of / 200. 100 MB from proof.ovh.net in 5.6 s (18.7 MB/s, QEMU's user network); registry.npmjs.org (TLS 1.2) 43 ms.

## Limits

TLS 1.3 and 1.2 (no TLS 1.1 or older, none of TLS 1.2's RSA key exchange, CBC or finite-field Diffie-Hellman suites), no session resumption, PSK, 0-RTT or renegotiation, no client certificates, no OCSP stapling, certificate transparency or revocation (OpenSSL does not check revocation by default either), no pinning (`certificateValidator:` can do it), no record_size_limit or max_fragment_length, key shares x25519 and secp256r1 only (no P-384 or finite-field groups), keys RSA and ECDSA P-256/P-384 (no P-521 or Ed25519 certificates), chains of at most 8, host names in ASCII. ChaCha20-Poly1305 is Smalltalk, about 50 MB/s; we prefer AES-GCM. The public-key arithmetic is not constant-time (docs/crypto.md). The public-key arithmetic is not constant-time (docs/crypto.md).
