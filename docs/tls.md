# TLS

A TLS 1.3 client in Smalltalk (RFC 8446), `System-TLS`, for where the VM has no OpenSSL: UEFI, where it is the only HTTPS there is. It rests on `System-Crypto` (hashes, HMAC, HKDF, AES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, X25519, P-256, RSA and ECDSA verification, SecureRandom; docs/crypto.md) and on System-TLS's certificates (`X509Certificate`, `CertificateValidator`, `TrustStore`). `TLSSocket` uses it under its unchanged API, so `HTTPClient` (and server-sent events over it) works on UEFI as it does hosted.

    c := TLSClientConnection on: (RemoteSocket connectTo: 'example.com' port: 443) host: 'example.com'.
    c handshakeUntil: Time millisecondClockValue + 10000.      "TLSError if it fails"
    c sendAll: request; receiveInto: buffer timeout: 5000; close.

    TLSSocket useSmalltalkTLS: true.                            "hosted: the Smalltalk TLS rather than OpenSSL"
    TLSSocket trustStore: (TrustStore new add: testCA; yourself). "trust just that (nil: TrustStore default)"

## How

- **The connection.** `TLSClientConnection` runs over any connected byte socket that answers `sendAll:`, `receiveInto:timeout:` (0 at the end, nil after the timeout), `isOpen` and `close`: a `RemoteSocket`, over the OS's TCP or the image's (`NetworkStack`). It waits only on the socket, so its Process waits on Semaphores and the VM never blocks; the handshake has a deadline (a millisecondClockValue); terminating the Process that waits cancels. Before the handshake: `trustStore:` or `certificateValidator:`, `protocols:` (ALPN, `http/1.1` by default), `cipherSuites:`, `keyShareGroups:`, `recordPadding:`. After it: `sendAll:` (false once the connection is gone), `receiveInto:timeout:`, `updateKeys`, `close` (close_notify), and `cipherSuite`, `protocol`, `peerCertificates`, `wasRetried`, `handshakeMilliseconds`. One Process receives at a time; sends are serialized (a Semaphore), also with the KeyUpdates that receiving answers.
- **ClientHello** (`TLSClientHello`, which also parses one): TLS 1.3 only (supported_versions), the suites TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256, TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256, TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 in that order, SNI unless the host is an IP address, supported_groups x25519 then secp256r1 with a key share for x25519, signature_algorithms rsa_pss_rsae_sha256/384, ecdsa_secp256r1_sha256, ecdsa_secp384r1_sha384 and rsa_pkcs1_sha256/384 (for certificates only: a CertificateVerify with them is illegal_parameter), ALPN, and a random 32-byte session id: middlebox compatibility (RFC 8446, D.4), so a change_cipher_spec goes out once before the second flight (or the second ClientHello) and the server's are skipped until its Finished.
- **The handshake** follows section 4 strictly: a HelloRetryRequest (`TLSServerHello isHelloRetryRequest`) gets a second ClientHello with a share for the group asked for and the cookie, the transcript restarting with message_hash; a second one, one for a group not offered or already shared, or one that changes nothing is fatal. ServerHello must echo the session id, choose TLS 1.3, a suite offered (the HelloRetryRequest's) and our group; then `TLSKeySchedule` (section 7.1) derives the handshake secrets from the (EC)DHE secret and the transcript hash, and records are protected. EncryptedExtensions may only answer what was offered (else unsupported_extension) and not what belongs in the hellos (illegal_parameter); ALPN must choose one of ours. A CertificateRequest is answered with an empty Certificate. Certificate (an empty request context, no entry extensions, at least one certificate) is parsed and validated for the host at the current time (`CertificateValidator`; its failures are bad_certificate); CertificateVerify is checked with the leaf's key over 64 spaces, the context string and the transcript hash (RSA-PSS or ECDSA by scheme, the key's type and curve matching); Finished by HMAC, in constant time. Then the client's Finished and the application keys. Handshake messages may span records but not key changes (ServerHello and Finished end their records); messages of more than 256 KB are refused.
- **Records** (`TLSRecordCipher`, one a direction and key): at most 2^14 bytes of plaintext a record (2^14 + 256 of ciphertext received: else record_overflow), the nonce the IV XOR the 64-bit sequence number, the header the additional data, the content type after the content, zero padding stripped on receipt (optional on sending, `recordPadding:`); a record that does not decrypt is bad_record_mac. Alerts: close_notify ends the input (receiving answers 0), user_canceled is ignored, any other is fatal (`TLSError`, `isFromPeer`). A failure the client detects sends the alert that says why (`TLSError alert`, encrypted once there are keys) and closes the socket; a timeout or a closed connection sends none. After the handshake: NewSessionTicket is ignored (no resumption), KeyUpdate updates the server's key (and answers with ours if it asked), anything else is unexpected_message. `TLSReader` and `TLSWriter` are the presentation language (decode_error on anything short or left over).
- **TLSSocket.** `TLSSocket connectTo:port:` connects a `RemoteSocket` and then uses OpenSSL's primitives (docs/platform.md) unless the socket is the image's own TCP (a negative handle: UEFI), `tlsStart` fails (UEFI's `UefiNoIO`), or `TLSSocket useSmalltalkTLS: true`; then a `TLSClientConnection` trusting `TLSSocket trustStore` (`TrustStore default`, the Mozilla roots, unless set). The connect timeout covers lookup, connect and handshake; a handshake failure is a `RemoteError` ("cannot connect to host:port: TLS handshake: why"); after it, a failed connection reads as ended (0) and sends answer false, as a RemoteSocket's do.
- **Entropy on UEFI** comes from the firmware's EFI_RNG_PROTOCOL (`SecureRandom`); QEMU's firmware has it with a virtio-rng device, which `make uefi-run` and the rest add.

## Tests

`Tests-TLS`, hosted: `TLSRFC8448Test`, the key schedule, traffic keys, Finished and record protection against RFC 8448's simple 1-RTT trace, and the whole client replaying the trace's ClientHello (whose private key the trace gives) to a scripted socket (`TLSTestSocket`) playing the server's records: it must send exactly the trace's records (Finished, application data, close_notify), also when the records arrive 7 bytes at a time or a message a record, follow a server KeyUpdate and answer it, update its own keys, and fail with the right alert on a corrupted record, Finished or CertificateVerify. `TLSClientConnectionTest`: the ClientHello's contents, HelloRetryRequest and change_cipher_spec, and a scripted server that does not speak TLS, speaks TLS 1.2, breaks the rules of ServerHello or HelloRetryRequest (session id, version, suite, extensions, key shares of the wrong size or of small order, a second HelloRetryRequest), sends alerts, overlong records or messages, closes, or stalls: each signals TLSError, sends the right alert or none, and closes. `TLSOpenSSLTest`: against `openssl s_server -tls1_3` with a CA and certificates made per run: each suite, a HelloRetryRequest (`-groups P-256`; and no key share at first), an ECDSA certificate, a CertificateRequest, an IP address, a wrong host name and an untrusted CA, 3 MB down, 2 MB up and back with KeyUpdates, HTTPClient and server-sent events over TLSSocket. `TLSSocketSmalltalkTest` runs TLSSocketTest (timeouts, cancelling, servers that are not TLS, handles left) with the Smalltalk TLS. `make test-uefi` gets `https://10.0.2.2:port/` from inside QEMU, from `tools/tls-test-server.sh` (a test CA, injected into the image by the eval, and an ECDSA certificate for 10.0.2.2).

## Performance

Hosted (arm64, the JIT), against openssl s_server on 127.0.0.1: a handshake with an RSA-2048 certificate 10 to 20 ms, with P-256 ECDSA 8 ms; 3 MB down in 108 ms (AES-128-GCM), 54 ms (ChaCha20-Poly1305), 96 ms (AES-256-GCM). On UEFI under QEMU (arm64, KVM): a handshake with the host's test server 29 ms; with https://inference-api.nvidia.com (X25519, AES-128-GCM, RSA-PSS, a chain of 3) 79 ms, its connect 103 ms with the lookup, then HTTPClient's GET of / 200.

## Limits

TLS 1.3 only (no TLS 1.2 servers), no session resumption, PSK or 0-RTT, no client certificates, no OCSP stapling, certificate transparency or revocation, no record_size_limit or max_fragment_length, key shares x25519 and secp256r1 only (no P-384 or finite-field groups), host names in ASCII. The public-key arithmetic is not constant-time (docs/crypto.md).
